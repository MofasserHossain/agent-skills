---
name: research
description: "Investigate a specific factual or technical uncertainty using primary sources and record reusable findings."
---

State the question and the decision it informs. Search relevant project sources first, then official documentation/source code or other primary evidence. Verify time-sensitive claims at the source.

Capture findings, source links, date, limitations and implications for the decision. Distinguish observed facts from inference. Reuse existing research when it still answers the question.

Save a focused note only when future sessions will benefit. Do not duplicate an entire manual or unrelated search results.

Independent research may be delegated when authorized and worth its overhead. Otherwise perform it directly. The output resolves a factual uncertainty or identifies the exact missing evidence; it does not silently make product decisions.

Bound the investigation by the decision: identify what evidence would support, rule out or leave the option unresolved. Check version, platform and operating conditions when applying a source to the project. When credible sources conflict, explain the mismatch and which claim remains uncertain rather than averaging their conclusions.

Stop when the evidence is sufficient for that decision or further progress needs a named missing source or experiment. Return the answer, supporting evidence, applicability limits and decision implication. A recommendation may follow from the findings, but distinguish it from a measured result; do not expand into an unrelated technology survey.
