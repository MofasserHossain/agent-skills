## Establish decisions

Read the brief, applicable project context and already settled decisions. Map which decisions depend on others.

## Resolve uncertainty

Investigate facts yourself. Ask the human about product intent, preferences and tradeoffs that evidence cannot resolve. Ask a small round of independent questions with recommendations; reserve dependent questions for later answers. Stop expanding the interview when remaining uncertainty cannot materially change scope, acceptance or implementation.

Use concrete examples and counterexamples. Separate rules, examples and unanswered questions. Research a named factual gap; prototype a named question requiring something runnable or visible.

## Record and finish

Record decisions and reasons where the feature keeps them. Define overloaded domain terms when they matter. Write a separate ADR only for a consequential tradeoff whose reason would otherwise be lost.

For efforts too uncertain for one session, keep a low-resolution decision map with resolved questions, open questions and blockers. Work one ready decision at a time; this is planning, not an implementation backlog.

Return the resolved scope and material unresolved decisions. Never supply the human's side of a HITL exchange.

Before ending, check that examples and counterexamples agree with the recorded rules, and each material open decision has a named owner or next investigation. An unanswered question is not agreement. Continue authorized work once its prerequisites are settled.
